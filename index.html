About me

I'm Shermaine Yap Shi Min, a Computing student specialising in Data Analytics at Asia Pacific University of Technology & Innovation (APU) in Kuala Lumpur.

The project I'm proudest of taught a network to forecast the next stroke in professional badminton rallies. It changed how I think about modelling: the clever architecture lost to a two-stroke window, and the honest baseline was the hardest thing to beat. I like work where the data is messy, the result has to be explained to someone, and the answer is allowed to be "simpler was better".

Right now I'm going deeper on applied AI and analytics: the University of Tokyo's GCI World data science programme, the Microsoft Power BI Data Analyst certificate, and building on AWS.

Optimisation & Deep Learning, APU, 2026

Used the ShuttleSet22 stroke-level dataset to predict a player's next shot type from the rally so far. Built a 1D-CNN and a Bi-LSTM against Random Forest and bigram baselines, ran eight controlled optimisation experiments plus a Keras Tuner random search, and landed on a GRU with a two-stroke window.

Press start. The model serves.

Each time the shuttle comes to you, the model locks in a guess for your next shot before you choose. If it guesses right, it reads you and wins the point. Get three shots past it in one rally and the point is yours.

The model is a small GRU trained on 22,349 strokes from professional singles matches (ShuttleSet22). On 9,909 held-out pro strokes it names the exact next shot 52% of the time and has it in its top three 94% of the time. It also keeps a private tally of your choices and leans on it more as you play.

Your shot options come from real play: only replies that pros actually use after the incoming shot.
